Why we built Opsbridge

When something breaks on site—power down, access issue, equipment fault—the work is urgent, but the coordination is usually messy. Teams jump between WhatsApp groups, phone calls, and spreadsheets. Context gets lost, the wrong people get pulled in, and there is no single place that ties the incident to the conversation.

Opsbridge exists to close that gap. It is built for organization admins, operations managers, supervisors, and technicians who need one system for day-to-day operations and one place to talk when it matters.

The problem it solves

Scattered communication

Incident chat lives outside the work system, so history and ownership are hard to find later.

Slow coordination

Managers struggle to reach the right on-shift people quickly when a site issue escalates.

Weak operational context

Sites, crews, shifts, and incidents sit in separate tools, so decisions miss key detail.

No shared incident thread

Each issue needs its own focused channel—not a noisy general chat that everyone ignores.

What the product does

Opsbridge is a multi-tenant operations hub. Teams manage their organization, sites, and crews; plan shifts and handovers; raise and track incidents; and coordinate in real time with chat, voice notes, and calls—without leaving the workflow.

  • Organization & access — multi-tenant workspaces, invitations, and role-based access for admins, managers, supervisors, and technicians.
  • Workforce operations — sites, teams, skills, availability, shifts, and shift handovers.
  • Incident management — create, assign, update status, attach evidence, and follow the issue through to resolution.
  • Operations center — dashboard visibility, notifications, and escalation policy so open work does not get buried.
  • Communications — channel-based messaging for sites/teams and people-to-people conversations, embedded next to the work.

Key use cases

1. Site incident response

A technician or supervisor raises an incident for a site. The team assigns an owner, discusses the issue in the incident channel, shares updates or voice notes from the field, and escalates with a call if needed—then closes the loop when the site is back online.

2. Day-to-day crew coordination

Managers and supervisors use site or team channels in Communications to keep the crew aligned during the shift, while direct messages handle quick one-to-one follow-ups.

3. Shift handover continuity

Outgoing and incoming supervisors hand over context in Opsbridge so the next shift starts with clear ownership—and can continue the conversation in the same operational channels.

Nexconn SDK features used

Real-time communication in Opsbridge is powered by the Nexconn SDK. These are the capabilities we rely on, and where they show up for users:

  • Group channels — shared conversations for incidents, sites, and teams inside Communications and the incident workspace.
  • Direct (1:1) channels — private messaging between people from the Communications directory.
  • Message history & live messaging — open a channel and continue the thread with full conversation history and real-time updates.
  • Voice notes — send short voice updates from the chat toolbar when typing is impractical on site.
  • Speech-to-text — transcribe voice messages in chat when the feature is enabled for the app.
  • Audio & video calls — start 1:1 or group calls from the chat header for urgent coordination.
  • Presence & connection awareness — clear connected/reconnecting state so users know when messaging is live.
  • User & group profiles — channel names, people names, and member counts shown in the chat experience.
